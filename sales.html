The buyer is allowed to leave.

A sales conversation respects the buyer when the offer is clear enough to refuse. Clarity is the work. Pressure is not.

Say the problem back

Listen until you can say the buyer's problem in the buyer's words. If they correct you, you do not have it yet. Do not rename their trouble into your vocabulary to make the offer sound inevitable.

Their words first. Yours after they agree you heard them.

Name the offer

State what you will do, what you will not do, what it costs, and how long it takes. Say it while a no is still easy. Then write down the same words you said, and send them, so the buyer is not translating you for a colleague.

I do not publish a rate card on this site. The work changes with the volume of talk and the state of the queue. The fee belongs in the first note, in writing, not in a hint.

Same words in the room and in the note.

Leave a real refusal

Say what a bad fit looks like. Some work belongs with someone else. Ask for a decision, and leave a real no on the table. The buyer may talk to other people, take a week, or stop. That is a finished conversation, not a failed one.

No invented scarcity. "I can only hold this until tonight" is allowed when tonight is a true constraint you would tell a friend. A mood is not a constraint.

A no is a complete outcome.

Offer study. Not a proposal to a real company. No brand names.

Situation. Someone sells a four-week pass on a company's public replies and on two sales calls a week.

The muddy version talks about presence and leaves out the task, the stop date, and the fee.

The study line: "For four weeks I edit the public lines and sit in on two sales calls a week. You keep the voice when I stop. I do not buy ads, and I do not run the accounts. The fee is in the first note."

The buyer can refuse that sentence. That is the point.
