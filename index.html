Answer like a person.

Benjamin Lopez Snyder consults on the talk a company uses in public, the way a sale treats the buyer, and the reply that follows when something has gone wrong.

What is the work?

I consult in social media marketing, sales, and customer support. Those are three jobs that share one standard: the company should sound like itself.

On the public side I build a voice and a posting practice. In sales I work on the conversation and on offer clarity. In support I design the queue, the tone, and the recovery. I am not a promotions factory. I do not keep a business-management ledger. The work is how the talk is conducted.

Voice, sales conduct, and support. That is the whole practice.

Who is it for?

Owners and small teams who already speak to customers, and who can hear that the posts, the pitch, and the help desk have drifted apart. If you want a pile of posts and no one accountable for the reply, I am the wrong person.

This page is his practice site, set in the form of an interview. It is not a client list.

People repeat what happened to them.

Why does support sit with the public voice?

Because people repeat what happened to them. A warm caption does not survive a reply that treats a customer as a number. Support is part of the public reputation, whether you meant to publish it or not.

The story of the reply travels farther than the post.

What do you refuse?

Invented urgency. A deadline is a date you can point to, not a mood. I do not publish audience totals or advertising returns as proof, and I do not put sample posts here that pretend to come from a real company. Short voice studies on this site are marked as studies. They use no brand names.

There is no picture of a social network on this site, and nothing here to count.

No praise wall. No false rush.

How fast do you answer?

Within two business days. That is the window I keep. If I am going to miss it, I write before it closes and name a time I can meet. I will not print a faster promise to look eager. When I ask a company to name a reply time, I ask for one they have already kept.

Two business days, said in advance.

Four standards

One voice
The public line, the sales note, and the support reply sound like the same organization. One person can read all three aloud without changing character.
A reply time you can keep
Name the window. Miss it in the open if you must. Do not print a promise you will not meet.
A sale that respects the buyer
Say what is for sale, what it costs, and what happens after a yes. The buyer is allowed to leave.
No fake urgency
A date on a calendar can be real. A countdown with nothing behind it is pressure, and pressure is not clarity.
