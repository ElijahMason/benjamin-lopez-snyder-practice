State the offer while a no is still easy.

A sales conversation respects the buyer. Say what is for sale, what it costs, and what happens after a yes. Then stop talking long enough for a refusal.

Say

  • The buyer's problem, in the buyer's words.
  • The task, the term, and the stop date.
  • The fee, in the first note, in writing.
  • What you will not do.
  • What a bad fit looks like.

Leave out

  • A deadline that is only a mood.
  • A hint that silence means yes.
  • A rival invented to speed the reply.
  • A different offer in the follow-up than the one you said aloud.

Offer study. Not a proposal. No brand names.

Clear line: "For four weeks I edit the public replies and listen to two sales calls a week. You keep the voice after I stop. I do not buy ads. The fee is in this note. You can refuse it."

If the buyer cannot refuse the sentence, it is not clear yet.

I do not print a rate card here. The fee depends on the volume of talk. It still belongs in the first note, not in a tease.
